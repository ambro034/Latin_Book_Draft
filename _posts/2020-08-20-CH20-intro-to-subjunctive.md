### Principles of Grammar

Many students of Latin are unnecessarily frightened by the thought of learning the subjunctive. There are some new endings to learn, but they are easier to remember (and far more regular) than the indicative endings you have already learned. One difficulty is that subjunctives typically come in dependent clauses, which are less common in English, but they are something you have already started to master. Another difficulty is just that subjunctive in English is less common. If you think more about the patterns than all the intricacies of the subjunctive, you will be able to understand it quickly.

Subjunctive is a **mood** like indicative or imperative. The main difference is that while indicative *indicates* what is true and imperative commands someone to do something, the subjunctive deals with possibilities.

Instead of describing what the subject *is, is doing, or was doing*, the subjunctive describes what the subject *may be, wants to do, or would have done in other circumstances*. Another way to describe it is that the relationship between the subject and verb is *objective* in the indicative (based on reality) and *subjective* in the subjunctive (based on possibilities).

This does not describe every subjunctive, but patterns can determine when you’re likely to see a subjunctive and (more importantly) how to translate it.

Independent Subjunctive

There are four major independent uses of the subjunctive. We’ll look at these first, even though dependent uses are more common (since that’s a primary job of the subjunctive).

These are:

1.  Hortatory and/or jussive subjunctive: polite commands. Technically hortatory describes verbs in the first person and jussive describes 2^nd^ and 3^rd^ person verbs.
    1.  We should go there tomorrow.
    2.  Let me find the treasure.
    3.  Let them read the books.
2.  Optative subjunctive: a wish or prayer. These show what the subjects hopes will happen. Note that these are always from the point of view of the wisher, but will have the person and number of the actual subjects. *I hope they will find us* is therefore third person plural (“they will find”) not first person.
    1.  I hope that we go there tomorrow.
    2.  I wish to find the treasure.
    3.  If only they’ll read the books.
3.  Deliberative subjunctive: an internal question or a statement of internal doubt. This is closely related to the hortatory/jussive, but it is directed inward by the speaker.
    1.  Should we go there tomorrow?
    2.  Shall I find the treasure?
    3.  Should they read the books?
4.  Potential: possible action. Also closely related to the other independent subjunctives, the potential describes what the subject may do or is likely to do.
    1.  We may go there tomorrow.
    2.  Perhaps we’ll find the treasure.
    3.  They should read the books.

\*The examples above are all translations of hypothetical present subjunctives. Translations of other tenses will be addressed in the next two chapters. Note too that the translations of the subjunctive tend to refer to the future. All subjunctive tenses tend to “peek” into a tense (so the imperfect subjunctive addresses the present, etc.). There is therefore no such thing as a future subjunctive.

### Principles of Latin

Whether the subjunctive is independent or dependent, the formation is the same. Because of the subjective relationship between the subject and verb, there is not always a straightforward translation. For this chapter, we’ll mainly use the potential subjunctive, but remember that there are other ways to translate the verb based on pattern and context.

##### Independent Subjunctive patterns

Without context, it is difficult to know how to translate independent subjunctives. At times, the translation is ambiguous and up for interpretation. There are some additional vocabulary words that tell you what pattern to expect. These words do not have to be translated; they simply show the pattern. These words and their patterns should be memorized as part of your vocabulary.

-   *utinam*: indicates the next subjunctive verb is **optative**
-   *forsitan*: indicates that the next subjunctive verb is **potential**
-   A question mark at the end of a sentence (when the primary verb is subjunctive) shows that an editor has decided it is **deliberative**
-   None of these words are required for optative, potential, or deliberative subjunctives; they are like the signs that tell you to look out for deer, a railroad crossing, or falling rocks. Signs aren’t required for a deer to cross, but they alert you to their presence.

##### Present subjunctive

Start with the mnemonic device *we beat a liar* (there are many variations available online, but any with the vowel pattern work just fine). To form the present subjunctive, simply replace the thematic vowel and add personal endings:

1^st^ conjugation (*ā*) -> *e*  
2^nd^ conjugation (*ē*) -> *ea*  
3^rd^ conjugation (*e*) -> *a  
*4^th^ conjugation (*ī*) -> *ia*  
\*\*\*(3^rd^ –io, like often, follows the pattern of 4^th^ conjugation)

| ##### Active | 1st conjugation | 2nd conjugation | 3rd conjugation | 4th conjugation | 3rd –io conjugation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st singular | voc**e**m | vid**ea**m | reg**a**m | aud**ia**m | cap**ia**m |
| 2nd singular | voc**ē**s | vid**eā**s | reg**a**s | aud**iā**s | cap**iā**s |
| 3rd singular | voc**e**t | vid**ea**t | reg**a**t | aud**ia**t | cap**ia**t |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | voc**ē**mus | vid**eā**mus | reg**a**mus | aud**iā**mus | cap**iā**mus |
| 2nd plural | voc**ē**tis | vid**eā**tis | reg**a**tis | aud**iā**tis | cap**iā**tis |
| 3rd plural | voc**e**nt | vid**ea**nt | reg**a**nt | aud**ia**nt | cap**ia**nt |

To form the present passive subjunctive, we simply “take the active endings off, and put the active endings on.” There are no exceptions or odd vowel changes like sometimes occur in the indicative.

| ##### Passive | 1st conjugation | 2nd conjugation | 3rd conjugation | 4th conjugation | 3rd –io conjugation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st singular | voc**e**r | vid**ea**r | reg**a**r | aud**ia**r | cap**ia**r |
| 2nd singular | voc**ē**ris | vid**eā**ris | reg**a**ris | aud**iā**ris | cap**iā**ris |
| 3rd singular | voc**e**tur | vid**ea**tur | reg**a**tur | aud**ia**tur | cap**ia**tur |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | voc**ē**mu | vid**eā**mur | reg**a**mur | aud**iā**mur | cap**iā**mur |
| 2nd plural | voc**ē**mini | vid**eā**mini | reg**a**mini | aud**iā**mini | cap**iā**mini |
| 3rd plural | voc**e**ntur | vid**ea**ntur | reg**a**ntur | aud**ia**ntur | cap**ia**ntur |

Below are possible ways to translate the present subjunctive:

-   This list is not exhaustive, and you’ll have to learn patterns to best decide which to use. This is just a general guideline
-   Note that the optative subjunctive is always from the speaker’s point of view, so *vocet* (3^rd^ singular) is “*I hope* she calls,” not “*she hopes* to call.”

*vocem*: I may call; I should call; I hope to call (**passive**: I may be called, etc.)  
*vocēs*: You may call; you should call; I hope you call  
*vocet*: He/she/it may call; he/she/it should call; I hope he/she/it calls

*vocēmus*: We may call; we should call; I hope we call  
*vocētis*: Y’all may call; y’all should call; I hope y’all call  
*vocent*: They may call; they should call; I hope they call

Although the translation of subjunctives is dependent on the sentence pattern, you can translate for now with the helping verb “might.” More patterns and translations will be introduced in the following chapters.

*vocarem*: I might call *vocarer*: I might be called

##### Perfect Subjunctive

Just like all verbs in the perfect system, perfect active subjunctive comes from the 3^rd^ principal part, while perfect passive comes from the 4^th^.

To form the perfect active, apply the tense marker *–eri—* and personal endings (you’ll note similarities to the future perfect indicative).

| ##### Active | 1st conjugation | 2nd conjugation | 3rd conjugation | 4th conjugation | 3rd –io conjugation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st singular | vocāv**eri**m | vīd**eri**m | rēx**eri**m | audīv**eri**m | cēp**eri**m |
| 2nd singular | vocāv**eri**s | vīd**eri**s | rēx**eri**s | audīv**eri**s | cēp**eri**s |
| 3rd singular | vocāv**eri**t | vīd**eri**t | rēx**eri**t | audīv**eri**t | cēp**eri**t |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | vocāv**eri**mus | vīd**eri**mus | rēx**eri**mus | audīv**eri**mus | cēp**eri**mus |
| 2nd plural | vocāv**eri**tis | vīd**eri**tis | rēx**eri**tis | audīv**eri**tis | cēp**eri**tis |
| 3rd plural | vocāv**eri**nt | vīd**eri**nt | rēx**eri**nt | audīv**eri**nt | cēp**eri**nt |

The perfect passive simply uses the fourth principal part (like the indicative). Its helping verb is just the subjunctive form of *sum* (see below).

| 3rd singular | vocātus, -a, -um _sit_ | vīsus, -a, -um _sit_ | rectus, -a, -um _sit_ | auditus, -a, -um _sit_ | captus, -a, -um _sit_ |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | vocātus, -a, -um _simus_ | vīsus, -a, -um _simus_ | rectus, -a, -um _simus_ | auditus, -a, -um _simus_ | captus, -a, -um _simus_ |
| 2nd plural | vocātus, -a, -um _sitis_ | vīsus, -a, -um _sitis_ | rectus, -a, -um _sitis_ | auditus, -a, -um _sitis_ | captus, -a, -um _sitis_ |
| 3rd plural | vocātus, -a, -um _sint_ | vīsus, -a, -um _sint_ | rectus, -a, -um _sint_ | auditus, -a, -um _sint_ | captus, -a, -um _sint_ |

Quite often the perfect subjunctive is translated the same as the indicative. Patterns will play a big role in how you translate, but for now, just use the simple translation:

*vocaverim*: I called *vocatus sum*: I have been called

##### Pluperfect Subjunctive

This is formed by adding *–isse* to the perfect active stem (it’s also the same form as the perfect active infinitive) and personal endings.

| ##### Active | 1st conjugation | 2nd conjugation | 3rd conjugation | 4th conjugation | 3rd –io conjugation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st singular | vocāv**isse**m | vīd**isse**m | rēx**isse**m | audīv**isse**m | cēp**isse**m |
| 2nd singular | vocāv**isse**s | vīd**isse**s | rēx**isse**s | audīv**isse**s | cēp**isse**s |
| 3rd singular | vocāv**isse**t | vīd**isse**t | rēx**isse**t | audīv**isse**t | cēp**isse**t |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | vocāv**isse**mus | vīd**isse**mus | rēx**isse**mus | audīv**isse**mus | cēp**isse**mus |
| 2nd plural | vocāv**isse**tis | vīd**isse**tis | rēx**isse**tis | audīv**isse**tis | cēp**isse**tis |
| 3rd plural | vocāv**isse**nt | vīd**isse**nt | rēx**isse**nt | audīv**isse**nt | cēp**isse**nt |

To form the pluperfect passive, use the imperfect subjunctive form of *sum*. This (like all imperfect subjunctives) is formed by adding a personal ending to the infinitives (*esse*).

| ##### Passive | 1st conjugation | 2nd conjugation | 3rd conjugation | 4th conjugation | 3rd –io conjugation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 3rd singular | vocātus, -a, -um _esset_ | vīsus, -a, -um _esset_ | rectus, -a, -um _esset_ | auditus, -a, -um _esset_ | captus, -a, -um _esset_ |
| Plural |  |  |  |  |  |
| 1st plural | vocātus, -a, -um _essemus_ | vīsus, -a, -um _essemus_ | rectus, -a, -um _essemus_ | auditus, -a, -um _essemus_ | captus, -a, -um _essemus_ |
| 2nd plural | vocātus, -a, -um _essetis_ | vīsus, -a, -um _essetis_ | rectus, -a, -um _essetis_ | auditus, -a, -um _essetis_ | captus, -a, -um _essetis_ |
| 3rd plural | vocātus, -a, -um _essent_ | vīsus, -a, -um _essent_ | rectus, -a, -um _essent_ | auditus, -a, -um _essent_ | captus, -a, -um _essent_ |

One common translation (but again, translation depends on the pattern) of the pluperfect is to show something that didn’t happen. In English, we just switch the normal pluperfect helping verb (*had*) with the subject, so instead of “I had,” we use “Had I.”

*vocavissem*: Had I called *vocatus essem*: Had I been called

                    `mood-new-${i}`
                ).value
            );

            q.newForms.some(function(answer){

                    newForm ===

};

>nec tua mactasset nodoso stipite, Theseu,  
>     ardua parte virum dextera, parte bovem;  
>nec tibi, quae reditus monstrarent, fila dedissem,  
>     fila per adductas saepe recepta manus.  
>non equidem miror, si stat victoria tecum  
>     strataque Cretaeam belua planxit humum.  
>non poterant figi praecordia ferrea cornu;  
>     ut te non tegeres, pectore tutus eras.  
>illic tu silices, illic adamanta tulisti,  
>     illic, qui silices, Thesea, vincat, habes.  
>Crudeles somni, quid me tenuistis inertem?  
>     aut semel aeterna nocte premenda fui.  
>vos quoque crudeles venti, nimiumque parati  
>     flaminaque in lacrimas officiosa meas;  
>dextera crudelis, quae me fratremque necavit,  
>     et data poscenti, nomen inane, fides:  
>in me iurarunt somnus ventusque fidesque:  
>     prodita sum causis una puella tribus.  
>Ergo ego nec lacrimas matris moritura videbo  
>     nec mea qui digitis lumina condat, erit.  
>spiritus infelix peregrinas ibit in auras  
>     nec positos artus unguet amica manus.  
>ossa superstabunt volucres inhumata marinae;  
>     haec sunt officiis digna sepulcra meis.  
>ibis Cecropios portus patriaque receptus,  
>     cum steteris urbis celsus in arce tuae  
>et bene narraris letum taurique virique  
>     sectaque per dubias saxea tecta vias;  
>me quoque narrato sola tellure relictam;  
>     non ego sum titulis subripienda tuis!  
>nec pater est Aegeus, nec tu Pittheidos Aethrae  
>     filius; auctores saxa fretumque tui.  
>Di facerent ut me summa de puppe videres,  
>     movisset vultus maesta figura tuos.  
>nunc quoque non oculis, sed qua potes, adspice mente  
>     haerentem scopulo quem vaga pulsat aqua;  
>adspice demissos lugentis more capillos  
>     et tunicas lacrimis sicut ab imbre graves!  
>corpus ut impulsae segetes aquilonibus horret  
>     litteraque articulo pressa tremente labat.  
>non te per meritum, quoniam male cessit, adoro;  
>     debita sit facto gratia nulla meo.  
>sed ne poena quidem. si non ego causa salutis,  
>     non tamen est cur sis tu mihi causa necis.  
>Has tibi plangendo lugubria pectora lassas  
>     infelix tendo trans freta lata manus;  
>hos tibi qui superant ostendo maesta capillos;  
>     per lacrimas oro, quas tua facta movent:  
>flecte ratem, Theseu, versoque relabere vento;  
>     si prius occidero, tu tamen ossa feres.
